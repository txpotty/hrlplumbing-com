    Jetting for Scale and Grease in Dublin

    Jetting uses water at high pressure to strip a pipe back to its original diameter. Homeowners around Dublin often install a softener to protect water heaters and cut down on spotting on fixtures. Mineral scale and grease build up on pipe walls over years and catch everything that goes down the drain, so a cleaned pipe stays clear much longer than one that was only cabled. Most jetting jobs take two hours.

    Downspout and Roof Drain Solutions in Dublin

    Gutters concentrate a lot of roof water into a few points, often right next to the foundation. Heavy clay soils are common in Ohio, and they hold water and shift with the seasons, stressing foundations and sewer lines. We pipe downspouts underground in solid PVC to a pop-up emitter or dry well well away from the house, with a cleanout at the base so the line can be flushed. One inch of rain on 1,000 square feet of roof is about 600 gallons. That is a lot of water.

    Restaurant and Industrial Drain Service in Dublin

    Restaurants and industrial buildings put more grease and solids down the drain than any house. Grease interceptors need pumping on a schedule. Kitchen lines get jetted between pump-outs, and we keep a service log for your inspector.

    Commercial Drainage Solutions in Dublin

    Businesses in Dublin lose real money when a floor drain backs up. We service floor drains, trench drains, grease lines and parking lot catch basins, and we schedule the work before opening or after close when we can. Jetting on a schedule prevents most emergencies.

    Sink, Tub and Toilet Drain Clearing in Dublin

    Kitchen sinks clogged with grease and bathroom drains slowed by hair make up most of our fixture calls. Pulling the trap is often the fastest fix. Toilets get a closet auger, and if that fails, we pull the toilet and clear it from the flange.

    Residential Drain Cleaning in Dublin

    Sump Pump Services in Dublin, OH

    A sump pump protects a basement or crawlspace from groundwater. Many sump floods come from a blocked discharge line, not a dead pump.
