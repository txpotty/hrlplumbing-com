    Pipe Replacement & Whole-Home Repiping in Dublin, OH

    Homes in Dublin, OH with galvanized steel or polybutylene tend to show the same symptoms: low pressure, rusty water and leaks that keep coming back. Repiping swaps every supply line from the main to each fixture for PEX or copper, usually in two to four days with water restored most nights. We patch every opening.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Dublin, OH

    Switching to tankless during a replacement is common in Dublin, OH, but it is a bigger job than a tank swap. A whole-home tankless unit can draw four times the gas of a 40-gallon tank, so the gas line from the meter usually needs to be upsized, and the vent changes from metal B-vent to stainless or PVC. Both are on the quote.

    Sewer Line Replacement in Dublin

    Sewer lines in Dublin fail from roots, cracks, collapses and bellies where the pipe sank and now holds waste. Heavy clay soils are common in Ohio, and they hold water and shift with the seasons, stressing foundations and sewer lines. We run a camera first.     Repair vs Replacement Cost Comparison in Dublin, OH

    A common rule of thumb says replace once a repair passes half the cost of new, or when the part is near the end of its life. Take a 12-year-old heater with a bad gas valve, where the valve alone costs a third of a new unit and the tank is already near the end. We price both.

    Well Pump & Pressure Tank Replacement in Dublin

    Rural homes around Dublin that run on a well go dry the moment the pump stops.     Gas Line Replacement in Dublin

    Old black iron or failing gas pipe in Dublin homes can leak at threaded joints, and a gas leak is not something to wait on. If you smell gas, leave the house, then call the gas utility from outside, and once the line is safe, we replace the damaged section or the whole run, pressure test it and relight every appliance. Testing comes before relight.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Dublin

    A well pump that clicks on and off constantly usually points to a failed pressure tank. A ruptured bladder lets the tank waterlog, the air cushion disappears, and the pump starts with every tap, wearing the motor out years early. A new tank fixes it. The pressure switch gets checked too.
