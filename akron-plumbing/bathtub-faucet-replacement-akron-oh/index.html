    Cartridge and Stem Replacement in Akron, OH

    Most dripping tub faucets need internals, not a new valve. We swap cartridges or stems and seats, greasing the O-rings so handles turn easily. Seat wrenches come out often. About an hour, usually.

    Thermostatic Tub Valve Installation in Akron, OH

    Thermostatic valves hold the temperature you set. Unlike a pressure-balance valve, which only reacts to pressure changes, a thermostatic valve senses the actual water temperature and adjusts the mix, which matters for kids and older adults who cannot react fast to a hot spike. Volume and temperature often get separate handles. They cost more than pressure-balance.

    Dripping Tub Faucet Replacement in Akron

    Drips at the spout come from inside the valve. Single-handle drips mean worn cartridge seals, and multi-handle drips mean worn washers and seats. Replacement parts stop it. Cracked bodies get a new valve.

    Emergency Tub Faucet Service in Akron, OH

    A tub that will not shut off needs water off now. Check behind the trim plate or an access panel for stops, and if there are none, shut the main and leave the drain open. A plumber heads out as soon as one is free. Broken stems are a common cause.

    Freestanding and Deck-Mount Tub Filler Installation in Akron

    Freestanding tubs need a floor-mount filler. The supply lines come up through the floor under the filler, which means cutting the slab or working from a crawlspace or basement, and the filler needs a mounting bracket anchored to the subfloor. Deck-mount fillers sit on the rim. Tub-only fillers need a temperature limit.

    Upgrading Tub Faucets in Akron, OH

    Plenty of people replace a working tub faucet for looks. Old cross handles can become a modern lever trim, and on a universal valve the wall never opens. We can match your sink finish. Delta and Moen make this simplest.

    Tub Faucet Repair vs Replacement Costs in Akron, OH

    A cartridge costs much less than a valve. Replacement inside the wall makes sense when parts are discontinued, the valve body is cracked or pitted, or you want anti-scald protection on an old three-handle faucet that has none. We quote both options. Most people choose repair first.
