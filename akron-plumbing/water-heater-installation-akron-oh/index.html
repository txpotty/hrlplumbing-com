    Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Akron

    Tank heaters still heat most homes' water, holding 40 to 75 gallons at temperature until you need it. We size the tank by first-hour rating, not just gallons, because that number tells you how much hot water it delivers in a busy morning. Gas or electric both work. The fuel hookup, the venting and haul-away are all part of the job.

    Electric Water Heater Installation in Akron, OH

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. Before hooking up the new tank we check that the breaker and wire size match the elements, since a mismatch is a common cause of tripped breakers and scorched terminals. They do not need a vent. You get more spots to put it.

    Gas Water Heater Replacement in Akron

    Swapping a gas water heater comes down to three connections done right, the gas, the vent and the water. Each gas swap gets a new connector, a drip leg, a draft test at the hood and a leak check with a detector at every fitting. Carbon monoxide is the risk. We follow code on all of it.

    Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Akron, OH

    Water on the garage floor and no hot water usually means the tank has rusted through. Shut the cold supply valve on top of the tank and turn the gas knob to pilot or flip the breaker, then call us. We carry common sizes. Most emergency swaps finish the same day.

    Electric Tank Water Heater Installation in Akron, OH

    Without gas service, an electric tank is the usual pick. We match the voltage and breaker size to your panel, and because electric tanks need no vent, they can go in a closet, an attic or a laundry room where a gas unit could not. Closets and attics get a pan. Quiet and simple.

    Gas Tank Water Heater Installation in Akron

    A gas tank is the most common water heater we install, in 40, 50 and 75 gallon sizes. Each one gets a new gas connector, a sediment trap and a shutoff valve, plus a vent connector pitched up toward the flue at a quarter inch per foot. Then a draft test. Big families do well with gas.

    Water Heater Expansion Tank Installation in Akron

    Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume water gains when it heats. Permits are commonly needed for water heater replacements, sewer repairs and gas line work, and HRL Plumbing handles the filing and inspections. An expansion tank gives that water somewhere to go, so the relief valve stops dripping and pipe joints stop taking pressure spikes every time the burner runs. We set the air charge to match your water pressure. It mounts on the cold inlet.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Akron

    A standard 40 to 50 gallon tank usually costs $1,600 to $3,500 installed. Tankless jobs run about $3,000 to $6,500 depending on gas line and vent needs.

    Tank water heaters last about 8 to 12 years, though hard water can shorten that. Regular flushing and anode rod checks help.

    Most jurisdictions require a permit for replacement. We pull permits when required and make sure the install meets code.

    Household size and peak demand decide the size. Two to four people usually do fine with a 40 or 50 gallon tank, while bigger families often need 60 to 80 gallons or a tankless unit sized by flow rate. Tankless goes by flow rate.

    It looks simple but involves gas, electrical and plumbing connections, plus a vent that has to carry exhaust out of the house every time the burner fires. Wrong installs cause leaks, carbon monoxide or electrical hazards. A licensed plumber is the safe call.

    Age, repair cost and efficiency decide it. If the heater is over ten years old or the repair costs more than half of a new one, replacing it usually makes more sense. Tank leaks cannot be fixed.

    Rusty hot water, rumbling from the tank, water at the base and an age past ten years are the big ones. Short hot water often points to heavy sediment or a broken dip tube. Any of those deserves a look.

    Start with correct sizing and a thermostat set at 120 degrees. Insulating the first few feet of hot pipe and flushing the tank once a year both help, and a tankless model cuts standby loss.
