    Ejector Pump Replacement in Cincinnati

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Cincinnati

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. Sump pumps push groundwater out to the yard, ejectors push sewage to the sewer, and only the ejector pit must be sealed and vented against gas. Never mix the two.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Cincinnati, OH

    Liberty builds simplex and duplex packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. That speeds up new installs, and their wide-angle float switches are less likely to hang up on the basin wall than older tethered floats, which is one of the most common reasons a pump fails to start. Stuck floats are common.

    Commercial Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Cincinnati, OH

    Businesses in Cincinnati, OH with restrooms, kitchens or break rooms below grade need ejector systems that keep up with heavier, steadier use than a house. A duplex system with an alternating panel is common, so if one pump fails the other keeps things running. Closed restrooms cost business. We size both pumps to carry the full load.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Cincinnati

    Residential ejector systems in Cincinnati serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Cincinnati

    Macerating systems like Saniflo add a bath fixture or two without breaking the concrete floor. It sits behind the toilet, grinds waste and pumps it through a small line, often three-quarter or one inch, to a drain above, which suits a small half bath. They need a vent too.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Cincinnati

    A high water alarm belongs on every ejector system, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. Sump pumps work hard during wet seasons, and a battery backup helps keep them running through storm-related power outages. Battery backup is an option.
