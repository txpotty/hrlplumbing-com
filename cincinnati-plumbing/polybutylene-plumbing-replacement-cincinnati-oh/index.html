    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Cincinnati

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Both are good choices. PEX usually costs less. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper can last longer. Type L copper can last 50 years or more in friendly water, and either one is a large step up from polybutylene.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Cracked fittings are a warning. Poly often looks fine right up until it fails, so if your home was built between about 1978 and 1995 and has this pipe, replacement is recommended even without a leak yet.

    It is not a good DIY project. Permits, testing and code all come into it. A hidden leak, a failed inspection or a voided pipe warranty can easily cost more than hiring a licensed crew.

    Both work well in Ohio homes. PEX bends, tolerates freezes and costs less. Copper is stiff, long lasting and fine in sun and heat, and we will help you choose based on the house, water and budget.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A and Type L copper with Viega ProPress or soldered fittings. We recommend materials based on your home and water conditions.
