    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Lorain

    Standard drain snaking in Lorain, OH usually costs $150 to $500 depending on where the clog sits and how easy it is to reach. Mains with heavy roots, or jobs that need jetting, run $400 to $1,000 and sometimes more. Camera work is extra. You get a firm price after the tech looks at the drain and before any cable goes in.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots chase that moisture to the joint and work their way inside. Once in, they grow quickly. In Lorain, clay with joints every couple of feet and old cast iron with lead and oakum joints get hit the most.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. Hair, wipes and roots respond well to it. Hydro jetting washes the entire inside of the pipe with high-pressure water, removing grease, scale and sludge the cable leaves on the walls. Repeat clogs usually need jetting.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Lorain's older homes suffer the worst of it. They also make our job riskier. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. Heavy main line root cutting or a long jetting run can take 3 to 6 hours. We tell you up front. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. Cables can kink, whip and wrap around a wrist in a second. They also get stuck. Getting a rental cable stuck in clay pipe can mean digging it out, which costs far more than a rooter visit.

    Routine drain cleaning in Lorain, OH does not usually need a permit. Digging up the yard, replacing the sewer or making a major repair usually requires one. We handle that paperwork. You will know about any permit before repair work is scheduled.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting leaves the pipe cleaner. Snaking handles one-time clogs, while jetting is for grease lines and scaled mains that keep coming back.
