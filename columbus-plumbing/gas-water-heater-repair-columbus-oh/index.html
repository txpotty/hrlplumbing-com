    Repair or Replace: Straight Answers in Columbus, OH

    Before any wrench turns, you get a written price for the repair and an honest read on the tank in your Columbus, OH home. A six-year-old heater with a bad thermocouple is worth fixing.     Gas Water Heater Noise Diagnosis in Columbus

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. A high whistle is different. That usually means a relief valve or a partly closed supply valve.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Columbus

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. The usual failures are the blower motor, the pressure switch and the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Then each part gets a meter test.

    Tank Leaks & Anode Rods in Columbus

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Homeowners around Columbus often install a softener to protect water heaters and reduce spotting on fixtures. The tech pulls the anode, checks what metal remains and swaps it if bare core wire shows. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Columbus home that has not started leaking from the shell. A wet bottom seam is different. That tank needs replacing.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Repair in Columbus, OH

    Restaurants and salons in Columbus, OH burn through hot water faster than any house, and a down heater stops the business.     Venting and Draft Repair in Columbus, OH

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. A loose, rusted or sagging section lets exhaust spill back into the house. We fix the vent. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.
