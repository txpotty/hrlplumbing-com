    Pump and tank work as a pair. A tank that is too small or has lost its air charge makes the pump start dozens of extra times a day, and every start heats the motor. Every pump job includes a tank check.

    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Columbus, OH

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. We replace the pump and prime it properly. After that, we watch the pressure gauge cycle from cut-in to cut-out.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Columbus, OH

    No-water calls often end at the control box, not the pump. On a three-wire submersible, the start capacitor or the relay in that box fails far more often than the motor down the well does. We test them first. Swapping a capacitor costs a fraction of pulling a pump.

    Solar Well Pump Systems in Columbus, OH

    Solar well pumps make sense for livestock tanks and cabins where running power from the meter would cost more than the pump itself. A few panels feed a DC pump through a controller while the sun is up. The storage tank does the job a battery would. Panel count comes from how many gallons you need a day.

    Control Box and Pressure Switch Replacement in Columbus, OH

    Not every dry faucet means a dead pump. A pressure switch with pitted contacts or a plugged sensing tube can stop the pump even though nothing is wrong down the well. The switch itself is a cheap part. We show you the burned contacts.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Columbus

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. The reading shows whether the fault is the motor or the wire.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Columbus

    Most well pump replacements in Columbus, OH land between $1,500 and $5,000. The spread comes from well depth and horsepower, and from whether you need a new tank or wire at the same time. You get it in writing first.

    Keep the tank precharge right and fix leaks fast. A tank that has lost its air charge makes the pump start far more often, and a dripping faucet or running toilet can keep a pump cycling all night. Sandy wells benefit from a filter or separator.

    Pump manufacturers usually warranty their units for 1 to 5 years against defects. A few manufacturers add time when a licensed installer sets the pump. We add a labor warranty of our own.

    A typical swap fills most of a working day. Deeper wells take longer because every 20-foot length of pipe has to come up, be unthreaded or unclamped, and go back down. You get a time estimate up front.

    Location is the difference. Jet pumps stay on the surface and suck water up from shallow wells, and submersibles sit in the well and push water up from far deeper. At depth, a submersible wins on efficiency.
