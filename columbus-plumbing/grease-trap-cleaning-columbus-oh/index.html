    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Lines in Columbus, OH

    A cable machine pokes a hole through grease. Jetting actually removes it. Our jetters run around 3,000 to 4,000 psi through a spinning nozzle, which scours the whole inside of the pipe and pushes the loosened grease downstream. Afterward a camera pass confirms the pipe is clean.

    Emergency Grease Trap Services in Columbus, OH

    Gray water coming up through the floor sinks in the middle of service is the classic sign the trap has hit capacity. Same-day trucks are the goal on emergency calls. First the trap gets pumped, and then we check the line past the outlet, since a lateral packed with grease will not clear just from pumping. Sometimes it needs jetting too.

    Compliance and Documentation in Columbus

    Pretreatment rules in Ohio are usually set and enforced at the city or sewer district level, and nearly all of them want proof of pumping kept on site. We leave a dated record every visit. It lists the gallons removed, the grease and solids depth before pumping, the condition of the baffles and tees, and the hauler that took the waste. A binder by the office door works fine.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Columbus

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Some cities set a fixed interval instead, often 90 days for interceptors. Whichever rule your program uses, we schedule to it. Changes are easy.

    Odor Control and Trap Inspections in Columbus

    A trap that stinks between pump-outs usually has a flattened lid gasket or a vent problem. We check both on every visit. A gasket costs little to replace, while a missing or broken vent can let sewer gas into the kitchen and also slow the trap's drainage, so that one is worth tracking down. Most smells get fixed that visit.

    Grease Line Jetting in Columbus

    Grease that slips past a full trap coats the 4-inch line out to the main. It builds up in rings, thickest at the bottom of the pipe. A snake cuts a narrow path through and the line plugs again within weeks, while a jetter with a rotating nozzle peels the grease off the whole pipe wall. A camera confirms it afterward.

    Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Columbus, OH

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. Every compartment gets opened and pumped to the bottom. With the tank empty, we inspect the tees, baffle wall, risers and lid seals, since a cracked riser lets dirt and groundwater into the tank. Lids go back on tight.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Columbus

    Most visits in Columbus run somewhere between $200 and $800, set mostly by trap size and how long it has gone since the last pump-out. Hard access adds time. Long hose runs, like 80 feet to reach a trap behind a walk-in, add to the price. You get the price first.

    No permit is needed just to have a trap pumped. You do need to keep the records. A new or resized trap is different, usually needing a plumbing permit and sometimes a review by the pretreatment program first.

    Two things, a full pump-out and a trap that drains properly when we leave. Skim-and-go is not how we work. If a drain problem traced to our visit shows up afterward, we send the crew back to fix it, and the depth readings on the sheet show what the trap looked like when we finished.

    It depends on trap size and menu. Most restaurants end up on a two-week to three-month interval. A home with a small under-sink unit might only need it once a year, while a busy fryer kitchen with a 50-gallon trap can need it every other week.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. Haulers call it brown grease. It is different from the yellow grease in your used fryer oil bin, which is recycled separately and should never be poured into the trap.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Generally a trap is the small unit indoors, often under 100 gallons. Interceptor usually means the large tank in the ground outside, often 500 gallons and up, and codes size the two in different ways.

    Grease passes the outlet and plugs the line. Then the backups start, and they tend to hit during the rush. On top of the cleanup, sewer districts can fine you and bill you for jetting the main, and sewage on the floor can get a kitchen closed by the health inspector.

    Zurn, Schier, Watts and Big Dipper units turn up across Ohio every week, along with Rockford traps and older steel boxes from makers that are long gone. We can usually find parts.
