    Comfort Height Toilet Installation in Columbus, OH

    Comfort height bowls stand about two inches taller than standard ones. Think about who will use it before you buy. Shorter adults and kids sometimes find the taller seat leaves their feet dangling, while anyone with bad knees or a hip replacement usually finds it much easier to sit and stand. Installation is the same as a standard bowl.

    Toilet Flange and Wax Ring Replacement in Columbus, OH

    The closet flange is the ring that ties the toilet to the drain, and it is the weak point on most old installs. On cast iron, the bolt slots rust out first. PVC and ABS flanges crack when someone overtightens the bolts, and a repair ring screwed over the top is often all it takes to restore a solid anchor. Seal goes on last.

    ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in Columbus

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. That spacing leaves room for a grab bar. Before anything is ordered we measure the drain, because shifting it to meet the ADA centerline means cutting into the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Smart Toilet Installation in Columbus, OH

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most need a GFCI outlet close enough for the cord. The water connection and mounting are ours, and when an outlet has to be added we schedule a licensed electrician instead of doing that wiring. Then we test every button.

    New Construction Toilet Installation in Columbus

    On new builds we set toilets after the finish floor is down, never before. A flange belongs flush with or just above finished flooring. An early set means the tile goes around the flange, leaving it low, and that calls for an extender ring later. Each bowl gets a fresh seal.

    Toilet Brands We Install in Columbus

    We install Kohler, TOTO, American Standard, Gerber and most other brands sold at supply houses and home centers. Every brand has its quirks. Some skirted TOTO models use a Unifit adapter, and certain Kohler tanks take a gasket that hardware stores rarely stock. We keep those parts on hand.

    Bathroom Remodel Toilet Installation in Columbus, OH

    New tile over old vinyl can raise a bathroom floor by half an inch or more. Suddenly the old flange is below the floor. Either an extender ring brings the flange up or a new flange goes in, and relocating a toilet on a slab means saw-cutting concrete to move the drain. Moving it on a wood floor is easier.

    Wall-Mounted Toilet Installation in Columbus

    Wall-mounted toilets hang off a steel carrier bolted to the floor inside the wall. The tank sits behind an access plate. Because the carrier carries all the weight, we anchor its feet to the slab or framing first and check it for plumb before the drywall or tile goes up. Plan on a remodel, not a swap.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Columbus

    Labor for a standard toilet installation in Columbus usually runs $250 to $600, not counting the toilet itself. Flange repairs add to that. A rotted flange or a soft subfloor can push the total higher, and we price that work before it starts instead of surprising you on the invoice.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. The trouble spots are flange height and bolt torque. A low flange needs an extender ring, and one extra turn on a nut can split the base, which is why new floors and wall-hung units should go to a pro.

    About two hours for most floor-mount toilets. Wall-mounted and smart toilets take longer. A wall-mounted unit needs its carrier framed and plumbed before the wall is finished, so it often spans two visits, and a smart toilet may wait on an electrician for the outlet.

    Two warranties apply. The toilet is covered by its maker. Parts usually get one to five years and the china often far longer, and our guarantee picks up any leak caused by the install.

    Watch the base and the tank. Water that reappears on the floor after you wipe it up points to a cracked bowl or a failed seal. A seal can be replaced for far less than a new toilet, so we check that first, but a crack in the china means replacement, since there is no lasting repair for cracked porcelain.

    Start with the rough-in, since that decides which models fit at all. Next comes bowl shape. An elongated bowl is roomier but adds about two inches of length, and that matters in a tight half bath. Check the MaP flush score too.

    It depends on the floor and the flange. A straight swap on a solid flange is a reasonable DIY job. Hire out new tile jobs, broken flanges, wall-hung carriers and relocations, since mistakes there hide under the floor.
