    Cast Iron Sewer Line Lining in Dayton, OH

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Commercial Trenchless Sewer Lining in Dayton, OH

    Office and retail buildings often have a cast iron building drain under the slab that has rotted along the bottom. Lining it from a cleanout avoids saw-cutting the floor inside an occupied tenant space. We coordinate around tenant hours. A typical commercial lining job runs one to three days, depending on footage and how many branch connections need to be reinstated with a cutter.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Dayton

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. Skip that step and the liner can wrinkle or fail to bond. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Cost Comparison of Trenchless vs Traditional in Dayton, OH

    Depth changes the math. A line two feet down in open lawn is often cheaper to dig than to line, but a lateral eight feet deep that runs under the driveway and a porch costs far more to excavate than to burst or line from two pits. We quote both ways. Then you pick.
