    Gas Line Testing on Tankless Installs in Dayton, OH

    Every gas connection on a tankless gets a soap test and a manometer reading at full fire before we leave the house. A line that holds pressure at rest can still sag under full load. Both readings get taken.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Dayton, OH

    Electric tankless water heaters skip the gas line entirely, but a whole-house model can need 120 to 160 amps on two to four double-pole breakers. Plenty of panels do not have room. We check panel capacity and wire gauge before quoting.

    Commercial Tankless Racks in Dayton, OH

    Restaurants and salons run tankless racks hard. A rack of four units can draw more than 600,000 BTU, so we confirm gas pressure at the far unit with every burner firing. Installs can happen after closing.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Dayton, OH

    Pulling a 50 gallon tank out of a garage frees up floor space and ends standby heat loss. Gas is the catch, since tankless units usually need 3/4-inch pipe or larger and a new vent. Before quoting, we check the meter and pipe run, and afterward we cap the old flue and seal the roof. Expect a full day.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Dayton, OH

    Commercial tankless setups in restaurants and offices often use two or more units on a manifold, with a controller that splits the load. One bad unit can come out while the others keep running. Health code sets the hot water temperature at the hand sinks and dish areas.

    Commercial Tankless Replacement in Dayton

    Cascade controls on a commercial rack decide which unit fires first, and after one unit is replaced they need rebalancing. Skip that and the new unit wears out first. Descaling gets scheduled by usage.

    Exterior Mount Tankless Units in Dayton, OH

    Exterior-mount tankless units hang on an outside wall and vent straight out the front, so there is no vent pipe to run. Sun and weather wear on the covers and wiring. Frozen pipes are a regular winter concern in Ohio, especially in crawlspaces, exterior walls and unheated garages. We check the freeze protection settings and the pipe cover kit on every outdoor swap. They are usually the easiest swap.
