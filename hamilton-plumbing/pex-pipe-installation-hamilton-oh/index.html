    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Hamilton

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Hamilton, OH

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in treated water attacks the plastic and the acetal fittings from the inside, so it can look fine from outside right up to the day a joint lets go. Insurers ask about it. Swapping it for PEX can help with insurance and with resale.

    Hydronic Heating with PEX in Hamilton

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from. Heating work calls for oxygen-barrier PEX, coated so oxygen cannot seep through the pipe wall and corrode the boiler, the circulator and the iron parts. Loop length matters as well. Run them too long and the last stretch never warms up.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Hamilton, OH

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. All three are code-listed for drinking water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.
