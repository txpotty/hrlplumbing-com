    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Hamilton

    Price depends on the source. A supply line or fitting repair usually runs a few hundred dollars. Crack injection, sump replacement and interior drain systems step up from there, with full perimeter drains or exterior waterproofing reaching several thousand dollars because of the concrete work and digging. We quote after testing.

    Zoeller, Liberty and Wayne cover most of our installs because they are durable and parts are easy to get. Primary pumps get cast iron housings. For homes that lose power in storms, we add a battery backup pump or a water-powered backup where city water pressure is strong enough, and we put a high-water alarm on the pit either way.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Polyurethane handles wet cracks. Interior drains use rigid perforated pipe in washed stone and a dimpled plastic membrane on the wall, which is more reliable than the flexible corrugated pipe some installers use because it holds its slope and can be jetted.

    Only some repairs need excavation. Outside membranes and new footing drains require digging to the footing. Pipe repairs, crack injection and interior drain systems are all done from inside the basement, which is why they are the more common choice on homes with patios, driveways or landscaping right against the foundation.

    Look for damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell, and white chalky deposits on concrete. Those deposits are efflorescence, left behind when water evaporates. A rising water bill or rust at the water heater base points toward a plumbing leak. Smell often comes first.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Clear them and extend the outlets away from the walls. Inside, pour a bucket of water into the sump pit every spring to make sure the pump kicks on, check the water heater and washer hoses each year, and replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel ones every five years or so.

    For sump pumps we mostly install Zoeller and Liberty, with Wayne for some battery backups. We prefer cast iron pump housings. For crack injection we use two-part polyurethane or epoxy kits from established commercial suppliers, and for interior drains we use perforated pipe with a dimpled wall membrane from the same manufacturer so the pieces fit together.

    It depends on the scope. Small pipe repairs usually skip the permit. Bigger work like a new stack or a backwater valve may need one, and we pull it.
