    Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Newark, OH

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin costs the least, often done in a few hours. A full new system with basin, floor excavation, roof vent, electrical and alarm costs more, but it is usually the only way to add a below-sewer bath besides a macerator. We price both options.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Newark

    Zoeller's sewage line covers home and heavier-duty use, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. The model comes from the pump curve for your lift, and most basement baths need a four-tenths or half horsepower unit that passes two-inch solids. Bigger lifts need more.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Newark

    Liberty ejector pumps show up on a lot of our installs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. A full-port valve gets added.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Newark

    Digging the pit means cutting the slab and excavating below it, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Tree roots and shifting clay often crack older clay or cast iron sewer laterals, leading to slow drains and backups. The basin sits on gravel with backfill around it so rising groundwater cannot float it, and new concrete seals the floor and locks it down. A floating basin breaks pipe.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Newark

    Residential ejector systems in Newark serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. Each system needs a sealed basin, a pump with a float, a check valve and a vent. Simple parts, exact install. Install mistakes cause more trouble than hardware, so the vent and lid seal get extra attention.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Newark

    A high water alarm belongs on every ejector system, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. We install audible alarms with their own power source, or Wi-Fi alarms that text your phone, so you hear about a stuck float while the basin is still filling and not after waste has reached the basement floor. Sump pumps work hard during wet seasons, and a battery backup helps keep them running through storm-related power outages. Battery backup is an option.
