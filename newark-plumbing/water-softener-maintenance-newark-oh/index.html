    Resin Rebedding in Newark

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. A resin cleaner is the first thing we try. If the bed is spent, we empty out the old resin and refill the tank with new high-capacity media, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Control Valve Diagnostics and Repair in Newark

    Leaks at the valve body, water running to drain around the clock, a motor that hums without moving or a display stuck on one cycle usually point to worn seals and spacers. On a Fleck 5600 the fix is a seal kit and a new piston, often done in under an hour without pulling the valve off the tank. It costs a fraction of a new control head.

    Yearly Water Softener Tune-Ups in Newark

    A tune-up starts with two hardness readings and ends with a full manual regeneration that we watch from start to finish. In between, we check the salt level, the brine well and the float. Figure on an hour for a typical unit.

    Softener Brands We Work On in Newark

    Most softeners sold in the last 20 years run on a handful of valve platforms, mainly Fleck, Clack, Autotrol and a few proprietary heads. Once you know the valve, the name on the cabinet matters less. Kinetico and Culligan build their own valves, so we check part availability before quoting.

    Hardness Testing at the Tap in Newark

    We test with a titration kit, counting drops until the sample changes color, because test strips are too rough for setting a valve. Raw water gets tested from an unsoftened hose bib or with the valve on bypass. The softened sample comes from a kitchen faucet.

    Full-System Softener Inspections in Newark

    An inspection covers more than the two tanks. The inlet and outlet piping, bypass, drain line and brine overflow all get traced and checked. Green crust on copper fittings, a slow leak at the bypass or a drain line jammed down into a standpipe all go on the written report.

    Breaking Up Salt Bridges in Newark

    To check for a bridge, we push a broom handle straight down through the salt. A hard stop, then a drop, means a bridge. We break the crust, pull out the mushy salt underneath with a wet-dry vac, then check the brine well, safety float, brine line and grid plate before refilling to about half full.

    Brine Tank Cleanout and Inspection in Newark, OH

    Brine tanks collect a gray sludge of salt fines and insoluble grit, and it builds up faster with cheap rock salt. The tank gets emptied, scrubbed and rinsed. Then we look for cracks near the overflow elbow and the brine well and refill with fresh salt.
