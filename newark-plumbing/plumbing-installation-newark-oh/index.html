    Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Newark, OH

    A basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste out. The fix is a sealed basin with an ejector pump that lifts waste to the main. We size the pump and basin to the fixtures it serves, vent the basin to code, and put a check valve and a full-port shutoff on the discharge so the pump can be pulled for service. Brands we trust here are Liberty and Zoeller.

    Expansion Tank and Sewage Ejector Installs in Newark, OH

    A closed water system has nowhere for heated water to expand, so pressure climbs every single time the heater cycles. An expansion tank absorbs that swing and keeps the relief valve from dripping. Homes with a PRV usually need one. Fixtures below the sewer line get an ejector system with a sealed basin, a check valve and a vent.

    PEX Repiping in Newark

    Copper repipes cost more and take longer, which is why most whole-house repipes today use PEX-A or PEX-B tubing with brass or poly fittings. You also hear less of it, since PEX softens water hammer. It also handles a freeze better. During deep cold snaps, homeowners around Newark can let faucets drip and open sink cabinets to keep heated air on the pipes. We use fittings matched to the tubing brand.

    Tank and Tankless Water Heater Installs in Newark

    Bradford White and Rheem both make good tanks, and the brand matters less than the fit.     Gas Line Installation for New Appliances in Newark, OH

    Water heaters, ranges, dryers, fireplaces and pool heaters all pull from the same gas service. Each branch is sized from the total BTU load and the run length so the last appliance on the line is not starved. New lines get pressure tested, always.     Natural Gas and Propane Lines in Newark, OH

    Natural gas and propane run at different pressures, and the pipe sizing changes with them. Our licensed plumbers size each run from the appliance BTU ratings and the developed length, then choose black iron or CSST to suit the route. Code requires CSST to be bonded. Skip the bond and a nearby lightning strike can arc through the tubing, which is one more reason gas work is not a weekend project.
