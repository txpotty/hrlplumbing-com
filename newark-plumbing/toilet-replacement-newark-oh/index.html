    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Newark, OH

    Toilets made before 1994 often use 3.5 gallons a flush, and some from the 1970s use five or more.     Toilet Rough-In Adjustments in Newark, OH

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, usually 12 inches, sometimes 10 or 14 in older homes. Take the measurement from the wall to the closet bolts, not from the baseboard trim. If the new toilet's rough-in does not match, it either sits too far out or will not fit. An offset flange can correct an inch or two without moving the drain.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in Newark

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. Some carriers need new bolts. We match the new bowl to the existing carrier whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.

    Cracked Toilet Replacement in Newark

    Porcelain cracks below the waterline are not safely repairable, and epoxy fixes on a tank under constant water pressure rarely last more than a few months. It needs to be replaced. We pull the cracked toilet and check the floor for water damage before setting the new one, since a slow crack leak often soaks the subfloor first.

    Toilet Brands We Replace in Newark

    Brand matters less than model, because every major maker sells a great toilet and a mediocre one at nearly the same price. We look at the flush valve size and MaP score. Parts availability counts too. A toilet with proprietary parts can turn a simple repair into a special order later.

    High-Efficiency Toilet (HET) Upgrades in Newark, OH

    High-efficiency toilets carry a WaterSense label and a MaP score, which tells you how many grams of waste the toilet clears in one flush. Look for 800 or higher. Higher numbers mean fewer double flushes and fewer plunger trips, and that is where the real water savings come from.
