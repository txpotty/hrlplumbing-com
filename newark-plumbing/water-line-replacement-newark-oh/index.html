    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Newark, OH

    Bursting fits old cast iron or galvanized lines that will shatter under the head. Pulling suits softer pipe like poly or copper. Either way the new pipe follows the old path, which matters when the route runs under a patio or a neighbor's fence, leaving you with two small holes instead of a long trench. Rock can stop it.

    Emergency Water Line Replacement in Newark

    Water bubbling up through the lawn or the street side of the sidewalk means a break, not a drip. First step, shut off the main valve. If that does not stop it, the break is on your side of the meter but before the valve, and we will shut it at the curb stop with a meter key and plan the replacement from there, usually starting the same or next day.

    Damage Assessment Before Replacement in Newark

    We start at the meter. If the little dial on the meter is spinning with every fixture off, water is leaving somewhere. From there we listen along the line with an acoustic ground mic, rule out a running toilet or water softener, and sometimes push a camera into the line when we suspect root damage or a crushed section, so the hole goes in the right spot.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Newark

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? A repair coupling is often the right call. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so replacing the whole run is cheaper over five years. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Newark

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years will wear through the pipe.     Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Upgrades in Newark, OH

    Street pressure varies a lot from one neighborhood to the next. Plumbing code caps house pressure at 80 psi. If your new line comes in above that, we add a pressure reducing valve right after the main shutoff, set it around 55 to 65 psi, and replace the gate valve with a quarter-turn ball valve. A quarter turn and the water is off.

    Water Line Materials We Install in Newark, OH

    Our trucks carry Type K copper, PEX-a and HDPE. Each has a place. Copper suits short runs and high pressure, PEX handles long runs with bends, and HDPE is the usual pick for trenchless pulls and bores, where we need a tough, fused pipe that can be dragged a long way. All of it is listed for drinking water, meeting NSF 61.
