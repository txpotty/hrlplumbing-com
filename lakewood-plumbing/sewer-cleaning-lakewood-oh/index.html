    Video Inspection Before Cleaning in Lakewood, OH

    Some lines need a look first. Old pipe especially. A camera run before cleaning shows whether the line has roots, a broken section or a grease plug, which tells us whether to cable, jet or stop and talk about repair.

    Cast Iron Sewer Cleaning in Lakewood, OH

    Cast iron rots from the inside. Newer homes around Lakewood typically use copper or PEX supply lines with PVC drains, which are easier to maintain. The bottom of the pipe wears first. Because a heavy cutter can punch through weak iron, we use chain descalers and a lower-pressure jet, then check the wall thickness on camera.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Lakewood

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Each fails in its own way. Roots love clay joints, while Orangeburg fiber pipe tends to squash into an oval and eventually collapse, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Hydro Jetting for Sewer Lines in Lakewood

    Jetting cleans the whole pipe wall. A cable only makes a path. Water at 3,000 to 4,000 PSI strips grease, scale and fine roots off the inside of the line and flushes it all toward the street, which leaves the pipe close to its original diameter.

    Shop and Restaurant Sewers in Lakewood

    Busy buildings clog more often. Volume is the reason. We bring larger jetters for 4 and 6 inch commercial lines and can work before opening or after closing, so the cleaning does not shut down a lunch rush or a full day of business.

    Sewer Camera Inspections in Lakewood, OH

    On a repeat backup, the camera goes in first. We would rather look than guess. A waterproof color camera on a push rod shows the inside of the line live on a screen, so we can see the clog, measure its distance from the cleanout and spot any crack or sag.

    Dealing with Grease Buildup in Lakewood, OH

    Grease hardens as it cools. Then it narrows the pipe. Kitchen grease coats the sewer line the same way plaque coats an artery, and over a few years the opening can close to the size of a finger, which is when drains start to back up, and jetting is the right fix.
