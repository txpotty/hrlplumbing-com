    Pressure Switch Replacement in Springfield

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. After swapping the switch and clearing the tube, we set cut-in and cut-out to match the tank, usually 40 and 60 psi. This is among the least expensive well repairs.

    Submersible Pump Replacement in Springfield

    Sand wears impellers down over time, and a pump that once filled the tank in a minute starts taking three. Other common failures are the motor, the splice and the check valve. Before pulling anything, we test the motor and wire from the wellhead. Replacement pumps get sized to the yield and depth of the well.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Springfield

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up, run quietly and last, but motors, impellers and splices still wear out.
