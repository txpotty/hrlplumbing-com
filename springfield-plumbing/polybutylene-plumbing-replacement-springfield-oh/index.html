    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Springfield

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper runs higher, and a yard line usually adds $1,500 to $4,000.

    Replacing a whole supply system takes special tools, code knowledge and real repipe experience. It takes skill and time. Hidden leaks, failed inspections and voided warranties are common results of DIY repipes, so this one is best left to licensed plumbers.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. We use Uponor most often. For copper, we install Type L tubing from established mills rather than the thinner Type M found in many older homes, because the thicker wall holds up better over time.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Cracked fittings are a warning. Poly often looks fine right up until it fails, so if your home was built between about 1978 and 1995 and has this pipe, replacement is recommended even without a leak yet.

    Private wells serve many rural homes, and iron, sulfur and hardness are common issues that call for filtration or softening. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. It adds scale. Scale buildup can reduce flow and add stress to pipe that is already brittle, so in hard water areas we often suggest adding a softener loop during the replacement.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Fittings and hot water lines often fail first.
