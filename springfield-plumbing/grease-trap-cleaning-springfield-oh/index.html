    Grease Trap Inspection and Repair in Springfield

    An empty trap is the only time anyone can see what is wrong with it. Cracked baffles and missing outlet tees are the usual finds. Without an outlet tee, the grease cap flows right into the sewer, and older concrete interceptors lose that tee more often than you would think. We can often fix small problems on the spot.

    Grease Trap Hydro-Jetting in Springfield, OH

    Some traps have years of grease baked onto the walls and inlet piping. Pumping alone leaves that crust in place. We jet the inside of the empty trap and the line feeding it, which takes the unit back to bare walls and restores the capacity the crust was eating up. Plan on about an extra hour.

    Indoor Grease Trap Cleaning in Springfield

    Indoor traps usually sit under the three-compartment sink or in a recessed pit near the dish station. Room to work is tight. We lay down mats, pump or hand-scoop the contents into sealed containers, and wipe down the floor before we leave so the kitchen can go straight back to prep. We also check the gasket and the flow control.

    Emergency Grease Trap Cleaning in Springfield, OH

    Sometimes the trap is fine and the problem is downstream. A camera tells us quickly. A trap that is half empty while floor sinks keep backing up points to a clog in the lateral toward the main, which needs jetting, not another pump-out. Jetting can happen the same trip.

    Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Springfield, OH

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. Every compartment gets opened and pumped to the bottom. While the tank is empty, we look at the inlet and outlet tees, the baffle wall, the risers and the lid seals, because a cracked riser lets groundwater and dirt in. Lids go back on tight.

    Fryer-heavy menus load a trap fastest. Two weeks is enough for a wings place to fill a 50-gallon trap.     Grease Trap Pumping and Hauling in Springfield

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Springfield

    Most visits in Springfield run somewhere between $200 and $800, set mostly by trap size and how long it has gone since the last pump-out. Access matters too. A trap behind a walk-in that needs 80 feet of hose costs more than one sitting next to the curb. We quote before starting.

    Slow sinks come first. Gurgling floor drains and a sour smell by the dish station usually follow. By the time gray water is coming up through a floor sink, the trap is usually well past 25 percent full and grease is already heading down the line.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. The industry calls it brown grease. It is different from the yellow grease in your used fryer oil bin, which is recycled separately and should never be poured into the trap.

    Staff can clean a small under-sink trap between pump-outs. Disposal still has to be legal. A common setup is daily skimming by staff and a full pump and scrape from us every few weeks, so the records stay complete.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. Disposing of it is where people get in trouble. Pretreatment programs often require a licensed hauler's record for each pump-out, so a kitchen that handles its own cleaning may have no paperwork to show when the inspector asks.

    There is no parts warranty on a pump-out, since nothing gets installed. We do guarantee the work itself. Should it drain poorly after our visit, or a backup trace back to something we missed, the crew returns and makes it right.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. Automatic grease removal units are on the list too. An AGRU uses a heater and skimmer wheel to move grease into a bin, and the bin, wheel and strainer all need regular cleaning.

    It depends on trap size and menu. Most restaurants end up on a two-week to three-month interval. Homes with a small unit may go a year, but a fryer-heavy kitchen on a 50-gallon trap might need pumping every other week.
