    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Replacement in Cuyahoga Falls

    Start with the meter. Close the main shutoff at the house and watch the dial. A moving dial with the main closed means the leak is outside. If it stops, the leak is inside, perhaps a toilet flapper or a pipe under the floor, and that is a different repair.

    In most places the utility owns the main in the street and the line up to the meter. Everything on your side of the meter is yours. Some cities draw the line at the curb stop instead, and we confirm the split with the water department. That decides who pays.

    Repair fixes one spot. We cut out the bad section and splice in new pipe. Replacement swaps the entire run from meter to house, which is the better choice once the old pipe is corroded everywhere or has failed more than once, since the next leak is usually a few feet away.

    Most homes take a day or two. Long rural runs take longer.

    Summer humidity causes condensation on cold pipes and toilets, which can drip onto basement ceilings and floors. Frozen pipes are a regular winter concern in Ohio, especially in crawlspaces, exterior walls and unheated garages. Burying the line deep enough is what keeps it from freezing. We set new service lines at or below the frost depth local code requires, insulate the riser where it comes up to the meter, and use pipe that tolerates the swings, which in cold country often means PEX.

    Less than you might think. Trenchless jobs leave two small holes. Open trench jobs leave a strip about a foot wide that we backfill, compact and cover with the original sod or new seed, and it is usually hard to spot after a few weeks.
