    Commercial Sump Pump Service in Parma

    Storm-Night Sump Pump Replacement in Parma, OH

    Most emergency sump calls come in at night, during the heaviest rain of the year, when the pump has been running nonstop for hours. We move those calls to the front of the line.     Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Parma

    Stuck floats and leaking check valves cause more sump failures than dead motors do. When the check valve leaks, the water in the pipe drops back into the basin and the pump has to move it twice, which shortens its life. Every pump we install gets a new check valve. Vertical switches suit tight pits, and tethered floats suit wide ones.

    Light Commercial Sump Pump Replacement in Parma, OH

    Light commercial work, think retail basements and small office buildings, usually means a 1/2 HP or larger cast iron pump with a 2-inch discharge and a panel alarm. We match the old pump or step it up. Grinder and ejector pumps are a separate job.

    Pedestal Pump Service in Parma, OH

    Louder, yes, but a pedestal motor sits high and dry, so in a narrow pit it often outlasts a submersible by several years. Narrow pits favor pedestals. Worn units get replaced, and we reset the float rod stops so the cycle is right. If your pit is wide enough, a submersible may serve you better.

    Yard Drainage Pit Pumps in Parma, OH

    Yard drainage pits and window well pumps can sit dry from fall to spring and then seize the first time a big storm fills them. We swap in a corrosion-resistant pump and clear out leaves and silt. Discharge goes away from the foundation. Test them each spring.

    Zoeller Switch and Pump Repair in Parma

    Zoeller units with a piggyback float plug the pump cord into the switch cord, and if that piggyback plug fails the pump never gets power. We test that first. Sometimes a new switch is all you need, and sometimes the motor windings are shot. We tell you which before we sell you anything.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Parma, OH

    A pit that is filling with the pump silent means you have maybe an hour or two before water crosses the basement floor, depending on how fast it is coming in. Check the breaker and the GFCI outlet first, then lift the lid and see whether the float is stuck. A wet vac buys time. Our trucks carry common sizes, so most emergency calls end with a new pump running the same visit.
