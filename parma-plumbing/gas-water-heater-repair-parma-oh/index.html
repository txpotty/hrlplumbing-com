    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Parma

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Parma, OH

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. We read the code, then test the thermopile and sensors before blaming the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. We leave the old valve with you if you ask.

    Gas Water Heater Code Checks in Parma, OH

    Fuel gas code covers more of a water heater repair than most people expect. We check for a sediment trap, an approved flex connector and a shutoff valve within reach of the unit. Garage installs in Parma, OH need the burner raised 18 inches off the floor unless the heater is listed as vapor resistant, and strapping goes on wherever the local code asks for it. Clearances get measured too.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in Parma, OH

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. Plenty of leaks come from the drain valve, the relief valve or a sweating supply nipple.     Venting and Draft Repair in Parma, OH

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. Sections that pull apart, rust through or sag let combustion gas spill into the room. The vent gets repaired. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Parma

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Parma home.
