    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Maintenance in Mansfield

    A standard inspection and cleaning typically runs $125 to $250. Replacing a float switch or check valve typically adds $75 to $250 for parts and labor.

    Much of Ohio has hard water, which leaves scale in water heaters, clogs aerators and shortens the life of appliances. Hard groundwater leaves mineral scale on the impeller, the float rod and the walls of the pit. Given a few years, the buildup slows the pump and can stick a vertical float so the pump never kicks on. Cleaning it off is part of every visit.

    Grinding, rattling, nonstop running, a pump that will not start, rust on the housing or damp spots on the floor near the pit. Each of those is reason enough to book a visit before the next storm.

    Not every house needs one. The houses that usually do have basements, crawlspaces, sunken garages or sit on low ground. Sump pumps work hard during wet seasons, and a battery backup helps keep them running through storm-related power outages.

    Without a working battery or water-powered backup, the pit keeps filling during an outage and eventually spills over onto the basement floor. During a heavy rain, that can happen in less than an hour. A water-powered unit runs on municipal pressure and is not an option for homes on a well.

    The check valve stops water in the discharge pipe from dropping back into the pit after the pump shuts off. Without it, the pump repeats its work. All that extra cycling shortens motor life and adds to the power bill.

    You can check that the pump is plugged in and the discharge outlet outside is not buried or blocked. Pouring a bucket of water into the pit shows whether the pump kicks on. What a homeowner check misses is amp draw, check valve wear and early float problems, which take a meter and pulling the pump to see.
