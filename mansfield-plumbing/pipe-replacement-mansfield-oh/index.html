    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Mansfield

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. Chlorine breaks it down over time. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, which can cause insurance headaches. Replace it.

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Mansfield

    Apartments and condos share risers. Shared lines mean shared problems. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, keeping each unit informed. Organized work.

    PEX Repiping in Mansfield

    PEX installs quicker. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, and home-run systems from a manifold let you shut off individual fixtures without killing water to the house. Popular choice.

    Slab Leak Pipe Repair and Replacement in Mansfield

    Water under a slab shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a sound of running water, or a meter that spins with everything off. We locate the leak first. Then you have a choice between breaking the slab to fix that spot or rerouting the line through walls or the attic, and rerouting often costs less and avoids future slab leaks on that line. We explain both.

    Whole-Home Repiping in Mansfield

    Repiping sounds bigger than it is. You can usually stay in the house. We work room by room, restore water most evenings, and keep dust down with plastic sheeting and drop cloths, then patch every opening before we leave. Clean finish.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Mansfield, OH

    Copper still has fans. It handles heat well and lasts decades. We install Type L copper for most interior supply lines, sweat or press the joints, while strapping each line properly, so the new pipe does not rattle or sag. Costs more than PEX.

    Corroded Pipe Replacement in Mansfield, OH

    Corrosion shows up as green or white crust on copper, rust on steel, or pinholes that drip. Some water is harder on pipe. We replace corroded sections or the whole system depending on how widespread the damage is, including fittings near the heater. Look at the joints.
