    Sediment Flushing in Canton, OH

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. That layer insulates water from the heat. We flush with full line pressure, opening and closing the cold supply in bursts to stir the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Canton, OH

    A maintenance plan puts your tank on the calendar so nobody has to remember. We call you when it is due. Plans cover one or two visits a year at a fixed rate, with priority scheduling if the heater fails between visits and a discount on parts like anodes and drain valves.     Gas Venting Tune-Up in Canton, OH

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. We check draft at the hood. Lint on the flame arrestor of an FVIR tank can starve the burner and kill the pilot. We vacuum it clean.

    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Canton, OH

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. Gas units get a burner, thermocouple and vent check. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Canton

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Those are fixable. Water on the floor is a different story, and the first question we ask is whether it is coming from a fitting on top or from under the jacket. Leaks from under the jacket mean the tank itself has failed.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Canton

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. A gauge on a hose bib tells us the static pressure. Then we lift the relief valve lever. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Canton

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Standard Tank Flushing in Canton

    Flushing is the one job every tank needs, gas or electric, new or old. The harder the water, the sooner it matters. We drain the tank completely, refill it partway, drain it again and repeat until the hose runs clear, which on a heavily scaled tank can take four or five cycles. Then we close the valve and check that it reseals without a drip.
