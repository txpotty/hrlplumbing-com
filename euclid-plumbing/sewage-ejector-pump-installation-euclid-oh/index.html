    Emergency Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Euclid

    An ejector failure often starts with the high water alarm, followed by slow drains and a smell near the pit. Stop all use of the basement fixtures and call, because once a plumber is on site most emergency replacements take just a few hours. Trucks stock popular models.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Euclid

    Liberty ejector pumps are a common pick on our jobs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. We still add a shutoff.

    Zoeller Ejector Pump Installation in Euclid, OH

    Zoeller has made ejector pumps for a very long time, and its cast iron models are the ones we see still running in older basements. They work well for new systems and for drop-in replacements, since many Zoeller models fit existing pits and discharge piping as is. Parts are easy to find.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Euclid, OH

    Liberty builds single and dual pump packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. That speeds up new installs, and their wide-angle float switches are less likely to hang up on the basin wall than older tethered floats, which is one of the most common reasons a pump fails to start. Stuck floats are common.

    Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Euclid, OH

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin is the least expensive option, often done in a few hours. A full new system with basin, floor excavation, roof vent, electrical and alarm costs more, but it is usually the only way to add a below-sewer bath besides a macerator. Ask for both numbers.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Euclid

    A high water alarm belongs on every ejector system, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. We install audible alarms with their own power source, or Wi-Fi alarms that text your phone, so you hear about a stuck float while the basin is still filling and not after waste has reached the basement floor. Older cities with combined sewers can back up during big storms, so a backwater valve can safeguard a finished basement. Battery backup is an option.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Euclid

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Euclid

    Digging the pit starts with a saw cut through the floor, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Tree roots and shifting clay often break into older clay or cast iron sewer laterals, leading to slow drains and backups. We bed the basin on gravel and backfill around it so it cannot float when groundwater rises, then pour new concrete to seal the floor and lock the basin in place. A floating basin breaks pipe.
