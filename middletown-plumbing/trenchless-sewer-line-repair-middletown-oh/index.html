    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacements in Middletown

    A bursting head is a steel cone, slightly wider than the old pipe, pulled through the line by a hydraulic unit or a winch cable. As it moves, it splits the old clay or cast iron and shoves the pieces into the surrounding soil while a string of new HDPE pipe follows right behind it. Fused joints, no gaps. This is the fix for collapsed pipe.

    Causes of Sewer Line Damage in Middletown, OH

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints pull apart or crack. Tree roots and shifting clay often crack older clay or cast iron sewer laterals, leading to slow drains and backups. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Then age finishes the job. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under load.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Middletown

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. A poor prep job means a wrinkled liner. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Depth changes the math. A line two feet down in open lawn is often cheaper to dig than to line, but a lateral eight feet deep that runs under the driveway and a porch costs far more to excavate than to burst or line from two pits. We quote both ways. Then you pick.

    Root Intrusion Repair in Middletown, OH

    Roots enter at the joints, where clay bells or cast iron hubs have a small gap or a failed gasket. Once in, they fill the pipe. We cut them out with a mechanical root cutter or a hydro jet, then line the pipe so those joints are sealed for good and the line stops needing a cleaning every few months. The roots cannot get back in.
