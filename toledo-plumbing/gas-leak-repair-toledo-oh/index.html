    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Toledo

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. We start with a gauge on the system to confirm there is a leak at all. Then the sniffer comes out. A combustible gas detector follows the reading from fitting to fitting until it spikes. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final answer.

    Gas Leak Detection in Toledo

    Some leaks announce themselves with a hiss you can hear across the room, and others lose a few cubic feet a day and only show up on the bill. Slow leaks are what the test gauge is for. We isolate, then sniff. We run the test twice, with appliance valves closed and then open, because some leaks only show downstream of a valve. Finding the exact joint keeps the repair small.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Toledo, OH

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. We add up the BTU load and measure the run length before choosing a pipe size. Undersized pipe starves burners. Where the old pipe is rusty or poorly threaded, we replace it with black iron or CSST, whichever reaches easier. A big tankless unit may also need a bigger meter from the utility.

    Gas Meter to House Line Repair in Toledo, OH

    The run from the utility meter to the house shutoff is the homeowner's pipe in most service areas, so the repair falls to a plumber and not the gas company. That stretch sees more weather than any other pipe in the system. Risers rust at grade. We replace the riser or the buried run, pressure test it and leave the meter connection ready for the utility to open the meter. Expect a half day for a typical riser swap.

    Permitting and Code Compliance in Toledo, OH

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. We tell you up front which way your job falls.     Interior Gas Pipe Repair in Toledo, OH

    Inside the house, gas leaks hide at unions in the basement and at the threaded nipple behind the furnace, places nobody looks. Testing runs from the meter inward, one section at a time.
