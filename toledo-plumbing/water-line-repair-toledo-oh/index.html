    What Our Water Line Service Covers in Toledo, OH

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Toledo, OH

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. No water means no open sign. HRL Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Toledo

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, while PEX gets a fresh crimp ring or expansion fitting with the right tool for the brand.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Toledo, OH

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. We show you the pipe we exposed.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Toledo

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. That is where trenchless pays off. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, either along the old route or through fresh soil next to it. Most replacements finish in a day. You also get the manufacturer warranty on the new pipe.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Toledo

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Rocks pressing against the pipe wear pinholes over time, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. We usually cut out two feet. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, then we look over the pipe on either side for more pitting before we backfill.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in Toledo, OH

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Free-running water washes soil out from under walks and slabs fast. HRL Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. Most breaks are shut down quickly. If the repair will run long, we rig a temporary bypass so the house has water while we work.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Toledo

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. We confirm it in about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and if it keeps creeping we know the leak is between the meter and the house. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Toledo, OH

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Private wells serve many rural homes, and iron, sulfur and hardness are frequent issues that call for filtration or softening. Scale builds up inside and the opening shrinks. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Toledo

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. Depth drives most of that. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often lands between $3,000 and $7,000 because of the equipment involved. The written price comes before we start.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. A full replacement is typically a one-day job. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, even with a few hours spent setting the pits and the rig. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    A loose fitting on an exposed hose bib, sure. Buried line repairs are another story. You need the leak located, 811 called to mark gas and electric, a safe trench and fittings approved for direct burial under the Ohio plumbing code. A bad joint underground means digging the same hole twice. Most homeowners hire it out.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, plus accidental hits from fence posts, irrigation trenching and utility work. Poor original installation is another, such as pipe laid on sharp rock.

    Labor is warrantied, always. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, and both terms are written on your estimate before any work starts. Keep the invoice. It is your proof of coverage.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, utility marking, careful excavation and burial-rated fittings. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Spot repairs are often exempt. The rules come from your city or county, so the answer can change from one town to the next. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.
