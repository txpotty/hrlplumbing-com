    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Toledo

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Ohio has a lot of older housing, and many homes still have galvanized supply pipe and cast iron drains that are wearing out. Slab homes without a basement rarely need a pump. Basements and below-grade rooms are a different case, since they can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the ground sheds rain rather than soaking it up. A cleanup costs more than a pit.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Tying a sump pump into the sanitary sewer is not allowed in most places, and it can draw a fine.

    A straight pump replacement generally goes without a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we check the local rules and pull permits when they apply.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Visible rust and a stuck float are other red flags, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Age matters too.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. Parts for all four are easy to find at supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. Common models ride on our trucks.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. A sticking float or rust streaks on the housing also mean trouble, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.
