    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Toledo

    Most replacements with a quality standard toilet, labor and disposal cost $450 to $950, and that covers the wax ring, bolts and a new supply line too. Premium one-piece or smart toilets can bring the total to $1,200 to $2,500. If the flange needs work, plan on another $100 to $250.

    Much of Ohio has hard water, which leaves scale in water heaters, clogs aerators and shortens the life of appliances. Hard water leaves calcium scale in the rim jets and on the flush valve seat, which slowly weakens the flush and makes flappers leak. Glazed trapways help. Our picks for hard water have glazed trapways and fill valves with fewer small ports.

    Watch for cracks, weekly clogs, a base that rocks after it has been reset, and a tank that uses 3.5 gallons or more. The age of the toilet matters too. A toilet more than 25 years old is usually a good candidate even if nothing is broken yet.

    The toilet's outlet has to line up with the drain, and the rough-in tells you which models will do that without moving pipes. Measure from the wall, not the baseboard. Most are 12 inches. If yours is 10 or 14 inches, only certain models will fit.

    About two hours for a standard swap. That covers removal, flange inspection, setting the new toilet and testing it, with the old fixture loaded on the truck at the end. A bad flange adds thirty to sixty minutes.

    Handy homeowners can do it, but the trouble starts when the old toilet comes off and the flange turns out to be cracked or sitting below the new floor. That is where most DIY installs go wrong. Overtightened closet bolts are the other common mistake, and that one cracks a new bowl.

    A like-for-like replacement in the same spot generally does not. A permit usually comes into play when drains move or a bathroom is added.

    Replacement wins when repair costs start to approach the price of a new toilet, or when the toilet is old enough to waste water every time it flushes. Cracks settle it. Frequent clogs and a rocking base point the same way.
