    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Cleveland

    A single clogged sink or tub in Cleveland, OH usually runs $200 to $350. Main sewer lines with roots or jetting tend to land between $400 and $1,000. Location changes the price. A clean outdoor cleanout keeps the price down, while pulling a toilet or working through a roof vent adds time. You get the number before we start.

    No permit is needed for routine rooting. A new cleanout or a pipe repair may need one. We pull permits for any repair work we do.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. A jetter uses water at thousands of psi to scour the inside of the pipe from wall to wall. Jetting cleans more. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. They hold up well to heavy daily use. Several sizes of cable and cutter ride on each truck, so the tech matches the tool to the pipe rather than using one setup everywhere.

    It can be, so we prepare for it. Drop cloths go down from the door to the work area, and we keep buckets and rags at the cleanout. Cables come out wet. It gets wiped as it comes out, and debris goes into bags before we clean up and leave.

    Slow drains, gurgling pipes and sewer smell are the early ones. If the tub fills when the toilet flushes, the main is blocked. That one needs a call. Several fixtures acting up at once almost always points to a main line clog rather than a single trap under one sink.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. A root-choked main or a long jetting job may take 3 to 6 hours. We tell you up front. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.

    Watch for very slow drains, gurgling from toilets and sour odors. Water coming up in the shower when something else drains is a classic main line symptom. Act on that quickly. The longer it goes, the more likely the next flush ends with sewage on the bathroom floor.
