    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Youngstown

    Costs range from about $200 for simple thawing to $1,500 or more for a burst pipe inside a finished wall. Where the pipe sits matters most. Open crawlspace lines are quick, and ceiling repairs take more time and drywall work.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Open flame is never safe here. If the frozen section is inside a wall, a ceiling or under the floor, call a plumber instead, because you cannot see whether it has already split, and thawing it can release water into the structure.

    Most thawing jobs take one to two hours. Burst repairs take one to four hours. Repairs inside finished ceilings or walls take longer because we open the drywall, repair the pipe, test it and leave the opening clean and ready for a patch, and multiple splits on one run add time as well.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Larger jobs can. Repiping a section, rerouting a line into a heated wall, or replacing a backflow preventer may require a permit in many jurisdictions, and we pull it when needed so the work is on record.

    No water at one faucet after a cold night is the classic sign. Frost on the pipe confirms it. Feel the pipe where it runs through a cold space, and if one section is much colder than the rest or you can see a bulge, that is likely where the ice is sitting.

    Disconnect hoses, cover outdoor faucets and backflow preventers, and let a faucet drip on very cold nights. Leave sink cabinet doors open. Away from home in cold weather, keep the heat at 55 or higher or shut off and drain the lines.

    Check which fixtures work. Ice is usually between a working fixture and a dead one. A dead sink on an outside wall usually means ice in that wall, which a thermal camera can confirm.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. The outside part drains when you close it. It only works if the hose is disconnected, because a connected hose traps water in the stem where it can freeze and split the valve inside the wall, which you may not notice until spring.
