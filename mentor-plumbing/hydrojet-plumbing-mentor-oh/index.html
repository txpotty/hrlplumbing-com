    Chemical-Free Drain Cleaning in Mentor, OH

    Hydrojetting uses only water, so nothing caustic goes into your pipes or septic tank. Caustic cleaners can harm old pipe. Jetting removes the buildup physically, and the debris flushes to the sewer or septic tank as normal waste, so there is nothing left in the pipe to eat at joints or harm the bacteria a septic system depends on.

    Storm and Yard Drain Jetting in Mentor, OH

    Yard drains, channel drains and downspout lines fill with sand, silt and leaves between storms. Older cities with combined sewers can back up during big storms, so a backwater valve can protect a finished basement. A clogged yard drain sends that water back toward the house, so we jet the lines from the outlet end and flush the catch basins before the wet season starts. We also check the pop-up emitters.

    Hydrojetting vs Snaking Cost Comparison in Mentor, OH

    Snaking a main line typically costs $200 to $450, while residential hydrojetting usually runs $400 to $900 with a camera inspection. The jet is the bigger bill. Because jetting removes the buildup that causes repeat clogs, many homeowners spend less over a few years than they would on repeated snaking visits for the same line.

    Sewer Lateral Jetting in Mentor, OH

    The lateral from your house to the street carries everything, so buildup there causes backups everywhere at once. We jet it from the cleanout to the tap. Low spots where the pipe has settled hold water and collect sludge, so we spend extra passes there and mark the location on the camera footage in case a repair makes sense later.

    Preventive Hydrojet Maintenance in Mentor

    Preventive jetting makes sense when the same line clogs more than once a year. Camera findings set the interval. A line with heavy grease might need jetting every six months, while a sewer with a few root entry points might only need it every 18 to 24 months, and the footage tells us which.

    Tree Root Removal with Hydrojetting in Mentor

    Roots find their way into sewer lines through cracked joints, and older clay pipe has a joint every few feet. We use a spinning root-cutter nozzle. The jet then flushes the debris to the main, and the camera marks the joints that let the roots in.
