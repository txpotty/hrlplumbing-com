    Water Line Repairs We Handle in Mentor, OH

    PEX Water Line Repair in Mentor, OH

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rodents chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Mentor

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, then swap a failed regulator for a new Watts or Zurn Wilkins valve when it reads too high. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Mentor, OH

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patching that kind of pipe tends to buy months, not years. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, typically finishing in one day whether we trench or pull the pipe. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Main Water Line Repair in Mentor

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Mentor, OH

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Much of Ohio has hard water, which leaves scale in water heaters, clogs aerators and shortens the life of appliances. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, and so is pressure that falls off when the shower and the washer run together. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Mentor

    Underground leaks hide well. The first clue is often a water bill that jumped for no reason. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Mentor, OH

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Two or more leaks on galvanized is a replacement. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. We show you the pipe we exposed.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Mentor

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. Depth drives most of that. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often lands between $3,000 and $7,000 because of the equipment involved. The written price comes before we start.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. A full replacement is typically a one-day job. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, even with a few hours spent setting the pits and the rig. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Know where the pipe runs. Frozen pipes are a regular winter concern in Ohio, especially in crawlspaces, exterior walls and unheated garages. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and readings above 80 psi mean the line is under more stress than it should be. On galvanized lines, budgeting for a new line is cheaper than an emergency.

    We back our workmanship. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, and both terms are written on your estimate before any work starts. Keep the invoice. It is your proof of coverage.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. A hiss at the meter is a giveaway. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is.
